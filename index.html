What I would want to learn first

Before suggesting anything for ATG, I would listen. These are the questions I would bring to the first conversations with People, Finance, Legal, IT, and regional leaders. Every sample approach on this page should be read as a hypothesis these answers would confirm, change, or replace.

Priorities and context

  1. What does the team most need to be true in the first six months, and how will we know it is?
  2. Which decisions sit with People Operations, and which sit with Finance, Legal, or the regions?
  3. What has already been started that I should build on and not repeat?

Compliance and risk

  1. Which compliance areas already have a clear owner, and which do not?
  2. Where do the entities and regions already work well, and what can be learned from them?
  3. Which advisers and providers are already in place for legal, tax, and benefits questions?

Systems and data

  1. Which systems hold employee data today, and who trusts which one?
  2. Which reports do leaders actually use, and which ones are rebuilt by hand?
  3. What would make a change in a system feel safe to the teams who rely on it?

Employee experience and ways of working

  1. What do employees in each region say is working, and what is hard to find or slow?
  2. How do questions reach the People team today, and where do they get stuck?
  3. What does flexible-first look like for different roles, and where does it need guardrails?

Team and capacity

  1. What does the team look like today, and where is the workload heaviest?
  2. What does good communication look like between People Operations and the business?
  3. What would you want me to leave alone, at least at first?

    Guardrails

    • Automation reminds, routes, and records. People decide and sign off on filings, I-9 corrections, and anything with legal exposure.

              How I scope AI work: a business requirements document (BRD)

              Before I build or switch on any AI tool, I write a short BRD. It is the plain-language agreement on what the tool is for, what it may touch, who approves what, and how we will know it worked. The sample below is an illustrative template using the Compliance Audit Assistant as the example, so the sections and wording are mine but the entries are not ATG's.

              BBusinessRRequirementsDDocument

              New to the term? A BRD is a short, plain-language page written before anything gets built. It spells out what we are trying to fix, what the tool should and should not do, and how we will know it worked. Think of it as a recipe card or a creative brief: everyone agrees on the dish before anyone starts cooking.

              📝 What it isOne page that Legal, Security, Finance and People Ops can all read and agree on.
              🙅 What it is notA technical spec or a long contract. No jargon needed to read it.
              ⏱️ When it happensBefore the build, so surprises show up early and cheaply.

                                • No required event outside local working hours, and every live event is recorded with a written recap.
